## Key Findings

* The highest average property prices were recorded in **Lagos Island (₦325.6M)** and **Lagos Mainland (₦306.4M)**, followed by **Abuja (₦274.3M)** and **Enugu State (₦270M)**. Lower average prices were recorded in **Kaduna, Ogun, and Edo States**.

* **Lagos Island dominated the dataset**, with **968 listings**, followed by **Lagos Mainland (338)** and **Abuja (328)**. These three markets also accounted for the largest total listing values, with Lagos Island contributing approximately **₦315.2B**, Lagos Mainland **₦103.6B**, and Abuja **₦90.0B**.

* Bedrooms and bathrooms showed a **positive but relatively weak relationship with price**, with correlations of **0.280** and **0.251** respectively. However, average prices generally increased from smaller properties to larger **4–7 bedroom homes**, with 5-bedroom properties averaging approximately **₦441.5M** and 7-bedroom properties **₦482.5M**.

* **Semi-furnished properties had the highest average price (₦318.9M)**, followed by unfurnished properties at **₦298.8M**, while furnished properties had the lowest average at **₦243.6M**. This suggests that furnishing status alone does not necessarily correspond to a higher listing price.

* The analysis of listing promotion levels showed substantial differences in average prices. **Basic listings averaged ₦600M**, while Enterprise listings averaged **₦320.4M** and VIP listings **₦305M**. This indicates that premium listing or boost status does not consistently correspond to higher property prices.

* **Villas, mansions, penthouses, and duplexes** attracted the highest average prices, with **villas averaging about ₦650M** and **mansions ₦423.8M**.

* **Lagos** dominated the dataset and premium market, particularly **Lekki**, while **Abuja (FCT)** also recorded **high average prices** and substantial total listing value.

* Overall, **Lagos and Abuja** showed **stronger premium-market activity**, while many other regions had considerably lower average listing prices.

## Business Recommendations

* Sellers in **Lagos and Abuja** should take advantage of the stronger concentration of high-value properties in these markets by using competitive pricing, high-quality property photography, detailed descriptions, and targeted digital promotion.

* Sellers in lower-priced regions could use **localized pricing strategies and targeted marketing** to improve competitiveness rather than applying pricing strategies based on premium Lagos or Abuja markets.

* Property platforms and sellers should not assume that **furnishing status or paid listing promotion automatically translates into higher property prices**. Pricing strategies should instead consider location, property type, size, and other relevant property characteristics.

* For future analysis, collecting **listing dates, location-level demand indicators, and transaction or engagement data** would allow deeper analysis of market activity, demand trends, and the factors that influence actual sales performance.
